# 7. Silage

## What is silage?

- **Silage** (サイレージ): cut grass or corn that is sealed with the air pressed out and **fermented by lactic acid bacteria** so it keeps. It is easy to think of it as "pickled grass"
- Unlike hay, it does not need many sunny days, so it is easier to make in rainy Japan, and it keeps nutrients for a long time
- Farmers make a whole year's supply at once and take out a little each day
- The container or place where silage is made is called a **silo** (サイロ)

## How fermentation works

![How silage is made and fermented](images/silage.svg)

1. Right after packing and sealing, the plants are still breathing and use up the oxygen left inside (a few hours to a day)
2. When the oxygen is gone, **lactic acid bacteria** (乳酸菌) grow and turn the plant sugars into lactic acid
3. When the lactic acid brings the pH down to **about 4**, spoilage bacteria can no longer grow, and the silage becomes stable (3–6 weeks)
4. As long as no air gets in, it keeps for many months

![How pH falls after the silo is sealed](images/silage-ph.svg)

There are three keys to good silage:

- **Keep air out**: chop finely, pack it down hard, and seal it quickly
- **Enough sugar**: sugar feeds the lactic acid bacteria. Legumes have little sugar, so they are harder
- **The right moisture**: too wet or too dry both cause problems

Farmers sometimes add lactic acid bacteria (an inoculant) to help fermentation.

## Types of silage

| Type | Japanese | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corn silage (whole-crop) | コーンサイレージ | The whole corn plant — grain, stalk and leaves — is chopped. High in starch from the grain, so high in energy. Harvested when the kernels are turning hard and yellow |
| Grass silage | グラスサイレージ | Made from grass. The grass is dried a little (wilted) to lower the moisture before packing |
| Wrapped bale silage (baleage) | ラップサイレージ | Grass is rolled into a round bale and wrapped in many layers of film. The big white or black rolls you see in fields |
| Whole-crop rice silage | 稲WCS（稲発酵粗飼料） | Rice is cut with the grain and made into silage. Rice paddies can be used to grow it |
| Haylage | ヘイレージ | Grass silage with lower moisture (40–50%) |

## Types of silos

| Silo | Japanese | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bunker silo | バンカーサイロ | Concrete walls on three sides. Filled, packed down by heavy machines, and covered with plastic sheets. Common on large farms |
| Drive-over pile | スタックサイロ | Piled on the ground with no walls and covered with sheets |
| Tower silo | タワーサイロ | The traditional tower. Often seen in pictures of farms |
| Silo bag | バッグサイロ | Packed into a long plastic bag |
| Wrapped bale | ロールベール | Each bale is wrapped in film (wrapped bale silage) |

## How to make it (corn silage example)

1. When the corn kernels are turning hard and yellow, a forage harvester cuts the plants and chops them into 1–2 cm pieces
2. The corn is taken to the silo right away, spread in thin layers, and packed down again and again by heavy machines (to push out air)
3. On the same day, it is covered with plastic sheets and weighed down with tires or similar to seal it
4. It is left to ferment for at least 3 weeks, and better for a month or more, before feeding starts

Typical moisture is 65–70% for corn silage and 60–70% for grass silage.

## Bad silage

| Problem | Cause | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Butyric fermentation | Too wet, too little sugar, soil mixed in | Butyric acid bacteria grow and it smells terrible, like rotting. Cows do not eat it, and it can cause ketosis |
| Mold | Air gets in (not packed enough, holes in the sheet) | Nutrients are lost. Mold toxins (mycotoxins) harm the cows |
| Aerobic spoilage (secondary fermentation) | After opening, air lets yeasts and molds grow | It heats up, the smell changes, and cows stop eating it |

- To prevent **aerobic spoilage** (二次発酵), keep the open face small and flat, and take out a good thickness every day (about 15–30 cm as a guide, more in hot weather)
- Fix holes in the sheet as soon as you find them (crows and rats sometimes make them)

## How to tell good silage

| Check | Good | Bad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smell | Sweet and sour, like yogurt or pickles | Sharp vinegar smell, rotten smell (butyric acid), moldy |
| Color | Yellow-green to yellowish brown | Blackish, white or blue mold |
| Feel | Moist | Sticky (too wet), hot (aerobic spoilage) |
| pH | 4.2 or lower (corn: 3.8–4.0) | High |

## Summary

- Silage is "pickled grass": sealed without air and fermented by lactic acid bacteria. It becomes stable at about pH 4
- Corn silage is high in energy. Wrapped bale silage is the big white rolls in fields
- The keys are keeping air out, enough sugar and the right moisture. Mistakes cause butyric fermentation or mold
- After opening, take silage from the face every day to prevent aerobic spoilage
