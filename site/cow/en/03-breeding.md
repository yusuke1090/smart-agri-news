# 3. Breeding: one calf a year

## What you will learn

- Why a cow must calve to give milk
- What "one calf a year" means, and why it matters
- Heat (estrus) and the signs of heat
- Insemination, pregnancy and calving
- Numbers used to measure breeding performance

## A cow must calve to give milk

- After a cow gives birth, she starts giving milk (**lactation**, 泌乳)
- Milk yield is **highest about 6–8 weeks after calving** (the peak) and then slowly falls. A graph of this is called the **lactation curve** (泌乳曲線)
- When yield falls, farmers stop milking about two months before the next calving (**dry-off**, 乾乳). This rests the udder and prepares it for the next calving and lactation
- So, to keep giving milk, a cow needs to **calve almost every year**

## One calf a year

![One calf a year: the calendar](images/one-calf-a-year.svg)

- **One calf a year** (1年1産): each cow calves once a year. The goal is a **calving interval** (分娩間隔, the days from one calving to the next) of 365 days
- Pregnancy lasts about **280 days**. 365 − 280 = 85, so the cow must **become pregnant again within 85 days after calving**
- After calving, the uterus needs about 40 days to recover. So farmers wait 50–60 days before inseminating (the **voluntary waiting period**, VWP)
- That leaves only days 60–85 for insemination. Heat comes every 21 days, so there are only one or two chances
- Only about 30–40% of inseminations result in pregnancy. So "one calf a year" is hard in practice, and the average calving interval in Japan is over 400 days

### Problems with a long calving interval

| Problem | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Less milk | The low-yield period after the peak gets longer |
| Fewer calves | Fewer replacement heifers and fewer calves to sell |
| More disease | Cows get too fat late in lactation and get sick more easily at the next calving (Chapter 11) |

## Heat (estrus)

- **Heat** or **estrus** (発情): the time when a female will accept mating
- It comes about every **21 days** (18–24 days). This is the estrous cycle
- Heat is **short: about 10–18 hours**. High-yielding cows tend to have shorter and weaker heats
- Ovulation happens about 10–12 hours after heat ends
- Heat often starts at night, so it is easy to miss

### Signs of heat

| Sign | Japanese | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing heat | 乗られてもじっとしている | The surest sign. The cow stands still when other cows mount her |
| Mounting | ほかの牛に乗る | Cows in heat, or close to heat, mount other cows |
| Increased activity | よく歩く・動く | Steps increase to 2–4 times the normal level. Collars and pedometers detect this |
| Restlessness, bellowing | 落ち着かない・よく鳴く | |
| Clear mucus | 透明な粘液 | Clear, stringy mucus from the vulva |
| Swollen, red vulva | 陰部が腫れる | |
| Eats and milks a little less | 食べる量・乳が少し減る | |
| Messy hair at the tail head | 尾の付け根の毛が乱れる | Rubbed by mounting cows. Some farmers put paint or stickers on the tail head to check |

- The share of heats that are actually found is the **heat detection rate** (発情発見率). With eyes alone, farmers may find only about half of them, so sensors help (Chapter 12)

## Artificial insemination

- Almost all dairy cows in Japan become pregnant by **artificial insemination** (AI, 人工授精). A vet or AI technician puts frozen semen (in a thin tube called a straw) into the uterus
- The best time is said to be 12–18 hours after heat starts. A common rule: "If you see heat in the morning, inseminate in the evening. If you see it in the evening, inseminate the next morning" (the **AM-PM rule**)
- Semen is chosen from bulls (sires) with known genetic merit for milk yield, body type and disease resistance. Predictions from DNA (genomic evaluation) are also used
- **Sexed semen** (性選別精液) gives about a 90% chance of a female calf. Farmers use it when they want replacement heifers
- There is also a method that uses hormones to time ovulation, so cows can be inseminated on a fixed day (timed AI, for example Ovsynch)

## Pregnancy and calving

- Pregnancy (gestation) lasts about **280 days** (275–285 days)
- **Pregnancy check** (妊娠鑑定): about 30–40 days after insemination, a vet checks by feeling through the rectum or by ultrasound
- If heat comes again about 21 days after insemination, the cow is not pregnant
- As **calving** (分娩) gets close, the udder fills up, the area around the tail head becomes loose, body temperature drops by about 0.5 °C, and the cow becomes restless. Sensors can also give an alert (Chapter 12)
- The placenta (afterbirth) comes out within 12–24 hours after calving. If it does not, it is a retained placenta (Chapter 11)

## Breeding numbers

| Number | Japanese | Meaning | Rough target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calving interval | 分娩間隔 | Days from one calving to the next | 365–400 days (shorter is better) |
| Days open | 空胎日数 | Days from calving to pregnancy | 85–115 days |
| Days to first service | 初回授精日数 | Days from calving to the first insemination | 60–80 days |
| Conception rate | 受胎率 | Share of inseminations that result in pregnancy | 40% or more |
| Heat detection rate | 発情発見率 | Share of heats that are found | 60–70% or more |
| Pregnancy rate | 妊娠率 | Share of cows that could get pregnant in each 21 days that actually do (heat detection rate × conception rate) | 20% or more |
| Age at first calving | 初産月齢 | Age in months at the first calving | 22–24 months |

## Summary

- Milk comes after calving. It peaks 6–8 weeks after calving. Cows are dried off about two months before the next calving
- One calf a year = a 365-day calving interval. Pregnancy lasts 280 days, so the cow must get pregnant within 85 days after calving
- Heat comes every 21 days and lasts only 10–18 hours. Finding it is important, and sensors help
- Most cows are bred by AI, 12–18 hours after heat starts
