# 8. How milk is made, and milking

## What you will learn

- The structure of the udder
- How milk is made, and how it comes out
- The steps of milking
- Milk components and milk tests
- Milk that cannot be shipped

## The structure of the udder

- The **udder** (乳房) is divided into **four quarters** (分房): front left, front right, rear left and rear right
- Each quarter is separate, and their milk does not mix. Mastitis also happens quarter by quarter (Chapter 9)
- The rear quarters are bigger and give about 60% of the milk
- At the tip of each **teat** (乳頭) is the **teat canal** (乳頭口). It is normally closed by a muscle and a keratin plug, which keeps bacteria out

## How milk is made

- Milk is made in tiny sacs inside the udder that look like bunches of grapes (**alveoli**, 乳腺胞). The cells around each sac take materials from the blood and make milk
- To make 1 L of milk, **400–500 L of blood** flows through the udder

| Material (in the blood) | Milk component made |
| --- | --- |
| Glucose | Lactose (decides milk volume) |
| Acetate and others | Milk fat |
| Amino acids | Milk protein |

- Lactose pulls water into the milk, so the more glucose the cow makes (mostly from propionic acid in the rumen, Chapter 2), the more milk she gives

## How milk comes out

- When the teats are stimulated, the brain releases a hormone called **oxytocin** (オキシトシン). It makes the muscles around the alveoli squeeze, pushing the milk out (**milk let-down**, 射乳)
- Oxytocin takes about a minute to start working and lasts only a few minutes. So attach the milking unit **60–90 seconds after** stimulating the teats for the best milking
- If a cow is scared or in pain, adrenaline blocks oxytocin and milk does not come out well. Milking **quietly, with the same routine every time**, is important

## Milking steps

1. **Forestripping** (前搾り): squeeze 2–3 streams by hand from each teat and throw them away. This removes bacteria from the teat and lets you check for abnormal milk (clots, watery milk, blood). It also helps let-down
2. **Pre-dipping**: dip the teats in disinfectant and leave it for about 30 seconds
3. **Wiping**: wipe and dry the teats with one clean towel per cow (sharing towels spreads mastitis)
4. **Attach the milking unit**: 60–90 seconds after stimulation
5. **Milking**: about 5–8 minutes. When the flow gets low, the unit comes off automatically. Milking too long (overmilking) damages the teats
6. **Post-dipping**: right after milking, dip the teats in disinfectant. This keeps bacteria out until the teat canal closes (about 30 minutes to 1 hour)

- Most farms milk twice a day (every 12 hours). Milking three times a day gives about 10% more milk
- With a **milking robot** (automatic milking system, AMS), cows walk in by themselves and the robot finds the teats and milks them. Cows are milked 2.5–3 times a day on average
- Milk is cooled right away in a bulk tank (to about 4 °C)

## Milk components

| Component | Japanese | Typical for Holsteins | What changes it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water | 水分 | about 87% | |
| Milk fat | 乳脂肪 | 3.5–4.2% | Falls when there is not enough roughage (fiber) |
| Milk protein | 乳たんぱく質 | 3.1–3.4% | Falls when there is not enough energy |
| Lactose | 乳糖 | 4.5–4.8% | Almost constant |
| Solids-not-fat (SNF) | 無脂乳固形分 | 8.6–8.9% | Protein + lactose + minerals |

- The milk price depends on components (fat and SNF) and cleanliness (somatic cell count and bacteria count)
- If the fat percentage falls below the protein percentage (an "inversion"), rumen acidosis is suspected. If fat is very high (for example 5% or more) just after calving, ketosis is suspected (Chapter 11)

## Milk tests

| Test | Japanese | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Somatic cell count (SCC) | 体細胞数 | The number of cells (mostly white blood cells) in 1 mL of milk. A sign of mastitis. Healthy quarters have 100,000 or fewer. Over 200,000 suggests subclinical mastitis |
| Bacteria count | 細菌数 | Whether milking and cooling are hygienic |
| Antibiotic residue | 抗生物質の残留 | Whether milk from a treated cow got mixed in. If it did, the whole tank must be thrown away |

- **Milk recording** (牛群検定, also called DHI): once a month, the milk yield, components and SCC of each cow are measured. It is used to check each cow's performance and health

## Milk that cannot be shipped

- **Milk within 5 days after calving (colostrum)**: in Japan the law (乳等省令) says it cannot be shipped. It is fed to calves (Chapter 4)
- **Abnormal milk**: milk with mastitis clots, watery milk, blood in milk (Chapters 9 and 10)
- **Milk from cows treated with antibiotics**, and milk during the set time after treatment (the **withdrawal period**, 休薬期間)

## Summary

- The udder has four separate quarters
- Milk is made from blood (400–500 L of blood for 1 L of milk). Lactose decides how much milk there is
- Oxytocin lets milk down. Attach the unit 60–90 seconds after stimulation. Milk quietly, with the same routine
- Forestrip → disinfect → wipe → milk → post-dip
- SCC is a sign of mastitis. Colostrum, abnormal milk and milk in the withdrawal period are not shipped
