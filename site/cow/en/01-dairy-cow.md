# 1. What is a dairy cow?

## What you will learn

- The difference between dairy cattle and beef cattle
- Dairy breeds
- Typical body size and milk yield
- The life of a dairy cow
- What kind of animal a cow is

## Dairy cattle and beef cattle

| Type | Japanese | Purpose | Main breeds in Japan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dairy cattle | 乳牛（乳用種） | Milk | Holstein, Jersey |
| Beef cattle | 肉牛（肉用種） | Meat | Japanese Black (Wagyu) and others |
| Crossbred (F1) | 交雑種 | Meat | Holstein mother × Japanese Black father |

- Only females give milk. Male calves born to dairy cows are raised for meat
- Farmers often use Wagyu semen on dairy cows to get crossbred (F1) calves, which sell for a good price

## Dairy breeds

| Breed | Japanese | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holstein (Holstein-Friesian) | ホルスタイン | Black-and-white. Gives the most milk. Almost all dairy cows in Japan are Holsteins |
| Jersey | ジャージー | Brown and small (400–450 kg). Less milk, but rich, with about 5% fat. A little better in hot weather |
| Brown Swiss | ブラウンスイス | Large, grey-brown. Strong and long-lived. Milk is good for cheese |
| Guernsey | ガーンジー | Light brown and white. Milk is slightly yellow |

The numbers in this book are for Holsteins.

## Holsteins in numbers

![The body of a dairy cow (Holstein)](images/cow-parts.svg)

| Item | Typical value |
| --- | --- |
| Body weight (adult female) | 600–700 kg |
| Height at the shoulder | about 150 cm |
| Birth weight | 40–45 kg |
| Body temperature | 38.0–39.0 °C (above 39.5 °C is a fever) |
| Milk per day | 25–35 kg on average. Some cows give more than 40–50 kg at peak |
| Milk per year | about 8,000–10,000 kg |
| Feed per day | 20–25 kg of dry matter (weight without water). 40–60 kg including water |
| Water per day | 80–150 L (more on hot days and for high-yielding cows) |
| Lifespan | A cow can live about 20 years, but dairy cows usually leave the farm at 5–6 years old |

Milk yield is often counted over 305 days after one calving (the "305-day yield").

## The life of a dairy cow

![The life of a dairy cow: calving, milking and dry periods](images/cow-life.svg)

| Stage | Typical age | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Young calf | 0 to 2–3 months | Drinks colostrum, then grows on milk and calf feed (Chapter 4) |
| Heifer (育成牛) | 3 months to first calving | Grows. First insemination at 13–15 months (Chapter 3) |
| First calving (初産) | 22–24 months | Gives birth for the first time and starts giving milk |
| Cow (経産牛) | from 2 years | Repeats "calving → milking (about 305 days) → dry period (about 60 days) → calving" |
| Leaving the farm | around 5–6 years | Because of low milk, failure to get pregnant, disease or bad legs. Most go for meat |

- **A cow only gives milk after she has a calf.** So dairy cows have a calf almost every year ("one calf a year", Chapter 3)
- The rest period with no milking is called the **dry period** (乾乳期)
- Female calves are raised to become the next dairy cows. Farmers sometimes use **sexed semen** (性選別精液) to get more female calves

## What kind of animal is a cow?

- A cow is a grass-eating **ruminant** (反芻動物). It has four stomachs and brings food back to its mouth to chew again (Chapter 2)
- Cows live in **herds**. There is a social order, and stronger cows eat first
- Cows **handle cold well but heat badly**. They are comfortable at about 5–20 °C. When it is hot, they eat less and give less milk (Chapter 11)
- Their eyes are on the sides of the head, so they can see almost all around (about 330 degrees). They cannot see directly behind, so speak to a cow when you come from behind
- Cows like routine (the same times and the same steps). Sudden noise, sudden movement and rough handling reduce milk let-down (Chapter 8)
- Cows lie down and rest for 10–14 hours a day. Cows that rest well give more milk (Chapter 12)

## Summary

- Almost all dairy cows in Japan are Holsteins. They weigh 600–700 kg and give 8,000–10,000 kg of milk a year
- Cows give milk only after calving. They repeat "calving → milking → dry period"
- First insemination at 13–15 months, first calving at 22–24 months. Most cows leave the farm at 5–6 years old
- Cows are ruminants, live in herds and are weak in hot weather
