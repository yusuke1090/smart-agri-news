# 9. Mastitis (and E. coli mastitis)

## What you will learn

- What mastitis is

- The bacteria that cause it (contagious and environmental)
- How E. coli causes mastitis
- How to find it, and sensors

## What is mastitis?

- **Mastitis** (乳房炎): inflammation inside the udder (the mammary gland). Most cases happen when **bacteria enter through the teat canal**
- It is the most common and the most costly disease of dairy cows. Losses include less milk, milk that must be thrown away, treatment costs, losing cows, and a lower milk price (because of high SCC)
- Inside the udder it is about 38 °C and full of nutritious milk — a very easy place for bacteria to grow

## Clinical and subclinical mastitis

| Type | Japanese | What you see | How to find it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clinical mastitis | 臨床型乳房炎 | Clots (flakes) in the milk, watery or odd-colored milk. The quarter is swollen, hot or painful. In severe cases, fever and no appetite | Look at the milk when forestripping, feel the udder |
| Subclinical mastitis | 潜在性乳房炎 | Milk and udder look normal | Somatic cell count (SCC), CMT (PL test), electrical conductivity |

- Subclinical mastitis is said to be 15–40 times more common than clinical mastitis. Without anyone noticing, it lowers milk yield and can spread to other cows
- Clinical cases are graded as mild (abnormal milk only), moderate (the quarter is also swollen) or severe (the whole body is affected: fever, no appetite, cannot stand)

### CMT (California Mastitis Test)

![How to read the CMT](images/cmt.svg)

- Squeeze a little milk from each quarter into a paddle with four cups and mix it with the test liquid
- If there are many somatic cells, the mixture becomes a thick gel. How thick it gets shows how bad it is
- It is a quick test you can do on the farm. In Japan it is called the PL test (PL テスト)

## The bacteria that cause it

![How contagious and environmental mastitis spread](images/mastitis-routes.svg)

| Group | Example bacteria | Where they live | How they spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contagious | Staphylococcus aureus（黄色ブドウ球菌）, Streptococcus agalactiae（無乳性レンサ球菌）, Mycoplasma（マイコプラズマ） | Inside infected udders, in teat wounds | From cow to cow at milking, through milking units, hands and towels |
| Environmental | E. coli（大腸菌）, Klebsiella（クレブシエラ）, Streptococcus uberis and other environmental streps | Manure, bedding, mud, dirty water | The teat ends get dirty when cows lie down or walk |

- **Contagious bacteria** cause long-lasting infections that are hard to cure. Staph aureus often does not respond well to antibiotics, and Mycoplasma hardly responds at all and spreads through the herd. They are controlled by milking hygiene and separating infected cows
- **Environmental bacteria** are controlled by barn hygiene (bedding and manure). E. coli can suddenly become severe

## How E. coli causes mastitis

![How E. coli causes mastitis](images/ecoli-mastitis.svg)

### 1. Where E. coli lives

- E. coli (大腸菌) lives normally in the cow's gut, and **manure is full of it**
- It grows in bedding mixed with manure (sawdust, straw, recycled manure solids), in mud and in dirty water. It grows fastest when it is **hot and wet**

### 2. How it gets into the udder

- Cows lie down for 10–14 hours a day. When they lie down, the teat ends touch the bedding
- **Right after milking, the teat canal is open** (it takes about 30 minutes to 1 hour to close). If a cow lies down on dirty bedding then, bacteria get in easily
- Damaged or rough teat ends (for example from overmilking) let bacteria in more easily
- **The start of the dry period and the time around calving** are also risky (before the teat plug forms, and after it is lost)

### 3. What happens inside

1. E. coli that gets in grows quickly in the milk
2. To kill the bacteria, the body sends huge numbers of white blood cells (neutrophils) from the blood into the udder → **SCC rises sharply**
3. When the bacteria are killed and break apart, they release a poison called **endotoxin** (エンドトキシン, 内毒素)
4. Endotoxin causes strong inflammation, and if it enters the blood, it affects **the whole body**

### 4. Signs

- **Udder**: suddenly swollen, hot and hard
- **Milk**: watery and yellowish (like yellow water), with clots. Milk yield drops sharply
- **Whole body**: fever (40 °C or more), shivering, not eating, rumination stops, diarrhea, dehydration. In severe cases the cow cannot stand and may die of shock (peracute mastitis). If the temperature starts to fall below normal, that is a dangerous sign of shock
- How severe it gets depends more on **the cow's immune condition** than on the number of bacteria. Just after calving, the immune system is weak, so cases are more severe
- On the other hand, if the cow's immune system kills the bacteria quickly, many cases stay mild and clear up in a few days

### 5. Treatment

- **Call a vet right away.** With severe E. coli mastitis, every hour counts
- Examples of what a vet may do: give fluids (a drip) to fight dehydration and shock, anti-inflammatory drugs, antibiotics, calcium
- The quarter may be milked out often to remove milk full of toxins and bacteria (frequent stripping)
- If antibiotics are used, milk is not shipped until the withdrawal period is over (Chapter 8)

### 6. Prevention

| What to do | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Keep bedding dry and clean. Remove manure often, add or change bedding | E. coli grows in wet manure and bedding |
| Post-dip after milking | Protects the teat until the canal closes |
| Give fresh feed after milking so cows stay standing for 30 minutes to 1 hour | So they do not lie down before the teat canal closes |
| Clean and dry the teats before attaching the unit | So dirt and water do not carry bacteria in |
| Fight heat (fans, ventilation) | Heat makes bacteria grow and weakens cows |
| Keep the calving area clean | Cows are especially at risk around calving |
| Seal the teats at dry-off (teat sealant) | Keeps bacteria out during the dry period |
| Vaccines | In some countries, an E. coli (J5) vaccine is used to make cases less severe |

## Finding mastitis, and sensors

| Method | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Looking at milk when forestripping | Clinical mastitis (clots, watery milk, blood) |
| CMT (PL test) | High cell counts, quarter by quarter |
| SCC from milk recording | Subclinical mastitis, cow by cow |
| Electrical conductivity (milking robots, milk meters) | Mastitis milk has more salt (sodium and chloride), so electricity passes through it more easily |
| Sudden drop in milk yield, rumination, activity, body temperature | Severe mastitis that affects the whole body |

## Summary

- Mastitis is inflammation of the udder caused by bacteria entering through the teat canal. It is the most common and most costly disease of dairy cows
- There is clinical mastitis (you can see it) and subclinical mastitis (you cannot). Find subclinical cases with SCC and the CMT
- Bacteria are **contagious** (spread cow to cow, such as Staph aureus) or **environmental** (from manure and bedding, such as E. coli)
- E. coli: dirty, wet bedding → the open teat canal right after milking → bacteria grow in milk → they break apart and release endotoxin → the whole body is affected
- Prevent it with dry, clean bedding, post-dipping and keeping cows standing after milking. Call a vet right away in severe cases
