# 6. Hay and grasses: timothy and alfalfa

## What you will learn

- The two groups of forage plants (grasses and legumes)
- The features of timothy and alfalfa, and how they differ
- Other roughages
- How hay is made, and when to cut
- How to tell good hay

## Two groups of forage plants

| Group | Japanese | Features | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grasses | イネ科 | High in fiber, lower in protein and calcium | Timothy, orchardgrass, Italian ryegrass |
| Legumes | マメ科 | High in protein and calcium. Bacteria in root nodules take nitrogen from the air, so they need less fertilizer | Alfalfa, clover |

## Timothy

- **Timothy** (チモシー): a grass. Its Japanese name is オオアワガエリ
- It is a perennial (it grows for several years) and handles cold well. A lot is grown in Hokkaido, and some is imported from the USA and Canada
- Its seed head is long and shaped like a thin cylinder
- It is fairly high in fiber and low in protein (crude protein about 8–12%). It is low in calcium
- Cows like to eat it (good palatability). It is good for increasing rumination
- Good for: **dry cows, heifers and calves**. For dry cows, its low calcium helps prevent milk fever after calving (Chapter 11)
- But grass from fields that got a lot of manure as fertilizer can be high in potassium, so be careful with dry cows
- It is also well known as food for rabbits and horses

## Alfalfa

- **Alfalfa** (アルファルファ): a legume. In the UK and some other countries it is called **lucerne**. Its Japanese name is ムラサキウマゴヤシ
- It is a perennial with purple flowers, and its roots go deep (several meters). It is so nutritious that it is called the "Queen of Forages"
- It is high in **protein** (crude protein 17–22%) and **calcium** (1.2–1.5%), and it is easy to digest
- Good for: **high-yielding milking cows**. It lets farmers use less protein from concentrate
- Japan imports it mainly from the USA, as hay, as pressed square **hay cubes** (ヘイキューブ), or as pellets

### Cautions with alfalfa

- **Do not feed a lot to dry cows**: too much calcium and potassium makes it harder for the body to release calcium from the bones at calving, so milk fever (low blood calcium) becomes more likely (Chapter 11)
- **Lots of fresh alfalfa can cause bloat**: gas in the rumen turns into foam that the cow cannot burp out, and the belly swells (Chapter 11)
- **Most nutrients are in the leaves**: leaves fall off easily when drying, so handle it gently. Leafy alfalfa is the best quality

## Other roughages

| Name | Japanese | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orchardgrass | オーチャードグラス | Grass. Handles heat better than timothy. Common on Honshu |
| Italian ryegrass | イタリアンライグラス | Grass. Grows fast. Often sown in autumn and cut in spring |
| Sudangrass | スーダングラス | Grass. Grows in summer |
| Clover | クローバー | Legume. Often sown together with grasses |
| Rice straw | 稲わら | Low in nutrients, but adds fiber. For dry cows and heifers |
| Corn (maize) | トウモロコシ | Usually cut whole and made into silage (Chapter 7) |

## How hay is made

1. **Mow** the grass (with a mower)
2. **Spread it out to dry and turn it** (with a tedder). This needs about 3–4 sunny days
3. **Rake it into rows** (with a rake)
4. **Bale it** (with a baler) into square bales or round bales

- Hay must be dried to **15% moisture or less**. If it is stacked wet, it can go moldy, heat up and even catch fire
- Rain washes out nutrients and makes mold more likely. Japan has a lot of rain, so making hay is hard. Farmers often dry grass only a little and make silage instead (wrapped bale silage, Chapter 7)

## When to cut

![Cutting time, yield and quality](images/cutting-time.svg)

| Cutting time | Nutrients | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early (young grass) | Leafy, high in protein, easy to digest | Small |
| Late (after seed heads or flowers appear) | Tough stems, high in fiber, hard to digest | Large |

- Grasses are best cut when the seed heads come out (heading stage), and alfalfa when it starts to flower. This gives a good balance of amount and quality
- Fields are cut several times a year. The first cut is called 1番草 (first cutting), the second 2番草 (second cutting)

## How to tell good hay

| Check | Good | Bad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Color | Green | Brown or blackish (too much rain or sun) |
| Smell | Sweet smell of grass | Moldy or dusty |
| Leaves | Many leaves | Mostly stems, leaves have fallen off |
| Feel | Soft | Hard, prickly |
| Mold | None | White or black powder. Mold toxins (mycotoxins) are bad for cows and people |

Besides looking at it, farmers send samples for analysis (feed analysis) and use the results to design the diet.

## Summary

- Forage plants are **grasses** (high fiber, such as timothy) or **legumes** (high protein and calcium, such as alfalfa)
- **Timothy** suits dry cows, heifers and calves. **Alfalfa** suits milking cows
- With alfalfa, watch out for milk fever in dry cows and bloat from fresh plants
- Hay is dried to 15% moisture or less. The earlier it is cut, the higher the nutrients
