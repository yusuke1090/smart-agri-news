## A cow's day

| Activity | Typical time per day |
| --- | --- |
| Lying down and resting | 10–14 hours |
| Eating | 3–5 hours (in 9–14 meals) |
| Ruminating | 7–10 hours (mostly while lying down) |
| Drinking | about 30 minutes (80–150 L a day) |
| Milking, walking, etc. | 2–3 hours |

- Cows that "eat well, rest well and ruminate well" are healthy and give more milk. Keeping cows comfortable is called **cow comfort** (カウコンフォート)
- Standing too long hurts the hooves and reduces blood flow to the udder

## Types of barns

| Type | Japanese | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tie-stall | つなぎ飼い | Each cow is tied in her own place. Easy to care for each cow. Cows are milked in place with a pipeline milking system. Common on small and medium farms in Japan |
| Free-stall | フリーストール | Cows walk freely and lie down in single-cow beds (stalls). They are milked in a milking parlor or by a milking robot. Common on large farms |
| Free-barn (bedded pack) | フリーバーン | Cows move freely on one large bed with thick bedding and no dividers |
| Grazing (pasture) | 放牧 | Cows eat grass in the field. Low feed costs, and cows get exercise |

## A dairy farmer's day

| Time (example) | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 5:00 | Morning milking, feeding, checking the cows |
| 8:00 | Cleaning the barn (removing manure, adding bedding), feeding calves |
| 10:00 | Checking heats and inseminating, vet visits, hoof trimming |
| 13:00 | Field work (grass and corn), paperwork, rest |
| 16:00 | Evening milking, feeding, cleaning |
| 19:00 | Clearing up, a last check on the cows |

- Caring for cows has no days off. Calving often happens in the middle of the night
- Areas have support systems: dairy helpers (people who do the milking so farmers can rest), contractors (who do field work) and TMR centers
- The shortage of workers is a big reason smart dairy farming is spreading

## Sensors and robots

| Technology | Japanese | What it measures or does | What it tells us |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Activity monitor (collar, pedometer, ear tag) | 活動量計 | Uses an accelerometer to measure steps, movement and head movement | Heat (activity 2–4 times normal), rumination and eating time, illness |
| Rumination sensor | 反芻センサー | Jaw movement or chewing sounds | Less rumination → illness, calving, heat stress |
| Rumen bolus | 胃のセンサー（ボーラス） | Swallowed by the cow. Measures temperature and movement inside the reticulum | Fever, how often the cow drinks, calving coming soon |
| Vaginal / tail sensor | 体温・尾のセンサー | Body temperature, how the tail is raised | Calving coming soon (temperature drops, tail raised more often) |
| Milking robot (AMS), milk meter | 搾乳ロボット・ミルクメーター | Milk yield, electrical conductivity, milk color, somatic cells | Mastitis, blood in milk, changes in milk yield |
| Camera + AI | カメラと AI | Body shape, walking, face | Body condition score (BCS), lameness, identifying cows |
| Walk-over scale | 通過式体重計 | Weighs cows as they walk through | Growth, weight loss or gain |
| Climate sensor | 環境センサー | Calculates THI from temperature and humidity | Heat stress. Can turn fans and misting on automatically |
| Feeding robot, feed pusher | 給餌・エサ寄せロボット | Delivers feed, pushes it back to the cows | Cows eat more, and farmers save work |
| Automatic calf feeder | 哺乳ロボット | Gives each calf its own amount of milk | How much and how fast calves drink → early signs of calf disease |

### What matters with sensors

- Look at **the difference from that cow's normal**, not at a single number (for example: rumination time is 20% lower than usual)
- An alert is not a diagnosis. It is a reason for **a person to go and check the cow**
- Each cow's data is linked to the **individual ID number** on her ear tag (10 digits in Japan)
- Collecting data for years shows how the whole farm is doing (breeding, mastitis, feeding), and which cows tend to get sick
- Much of the "dairy and livestock" and "IoT and sensors" news on this site is about the technologies in this chapter

## Summary

- Cows rest 10–14 hours and ruminate 7–10 hours a day. Cows that rest well give more milk
- Barn types: tie-stall, free-stall, free-barn and grazing
- Dairy work has no days off and there are not enough workers, so sensors and robots are spreading
- Activity shows heat, rumination shows illness, electrical conductivity shows mastitis, body temperature shows calving, and THI shows heat stress
- An alert is not a diagnosis. Look at the difference from normal, and let a person check
