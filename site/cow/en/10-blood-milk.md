# 10. Blood in milk

## What you will learn

- What blood in milk is
- Why blood gets into milk
- How to tell the causes apart
- What to watch out for

## What is blood in milk?

- **Blood in milk** (血乳, also called bloody milk): milk mixed with blood, so it looks pink to red. Sometimes blood clots come out too
- Because it contains red blood cells, if you leave it in a glass or test tube, red material sinks to the bottom
- **Milk with blood cannot be shipped.** Even a little in the tank changes the color of all the milk

## Why blood gets into milk

Milk is made from blood (Chapter 8). Many very thin blood vessels (capillaries) run around the milk-making tissue. **When these vessels break, blood gets into the milk.** There are five main reasons they break.

### 1. Right after calving (the most common)

- Around calving, **a lot of blood and lymph suddenly flows into the udder** to start making milk
- This makes the udder very swollen and tight (**udder edema**, 乳房浮腫). It is strongest in first-calf heifers (初産牛) and high-yielding cows
- The pressure of the swelling, and the udder growing so quickly, make thin vessels break easily
- Most cases **clear up by themselves in a few days to about two weeks**

### 2. Injury to the udder

- The udder can bleed when it is stepped on by another cow or by the cow's own hind leg, hit on a fence or a step in the stall, or when a teat is stepped on
- Cows with large, low-hanging udders get injured more easily
- If **only one quarter suddenly gives bloody milk**, suspect an injury

### 3. How the cow is milked

- Vacuum that is too high, overmilking, and old liners (the rubber that touches the teat) can damage the teats and the inside of the udder

### 4. Mastitis

- Inflammation can damage blood vessels and let blood into the milk. This happens with severe mastitis (for example from E. coli or Staph aureus)
- If there is fever, swelling or clots, or the CMT reacts, suspect mastitis (Chapter 9)

### 5. Diseases that stop blood from clotting (rare)

- Some diseases and poisonings weaken the blood's ability to clot (for example by lowering platelets). One example is poisoning from eating bracken fern (ワラビ). Then the cow bleeds from other places too, not only the udder
- If you see nosebleeds, small spots of bleeding on the skin or eyes, or blood in the manure, call a vet right away

## Telling the causes apart

| What you see | Possible cause |
| --- | --- |
| Just after calving, first-calf heifer, strong udder swelling, several quarters | Broken capillaries after calving |
| Suddenly only one quarter, bruises or wounds on the udder | Injury |
| Fever, swelling, clots, watery milk, positive CMT | Mastitis |
| Bleeding from other places too, the cow is weak | A disease that stops blood from clotting |

## What to watch out for

- Do not ship bloody milk. Forestrip to find **which quarter** it comes from, and keep that milk separate
- Talk to a vet if you do not know the cause, if it lasts many days, or if the cow has a fever or seems weak
- Do not rub or hit the udder hard (it can make the bleeding worse)
- To prevent injuries, remove steps and sharp metal in the stalls, use non-slip floors, and do not crowd the cows
- To reduce udder swelling, do not give dry cows too much salt (sodium and potassium) in their feed
- Milking robots and some milk meters check milk color with a sensor and separate bloody milk automatically

## Summary

- Blood in milk happens when thin blood vessels around the milk-making tissue break. This milk cannot be shipped
- The most common cause is udder swelling right after calving (especially in first-calf heifers). Most cases clear up in a few days to two weeks
- Other causes: injury, the way the cow is milked, mastitis, and rarely diseases that stop blood from clotting
- Find which quarter it comes from. If it lasts or the cow has a fever, call a vet
