# 5. Feed basics: roughage, concentrate, compound feed

## What you will learn

- The difference between roughage and concentrate
- Types of concentrate
- What compound feed is
- TMR and component feeding
- Words about dry matter and nutrients

## Types of feed

![Types of feed](images/feed-types.svg)

| Type | Japanese | Features | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Roughage / forage** | 粗飼料（そしりょう） | High in fiber and bulky. Increases rumination and saliva and keeps the rumen healthy | Grass (fresh, hay, silage), corn silage, rice straw |
| **Concentrate** | 濃厚飼料（のうこうしりょう） | Low in fiber, high in energy or protein. Gives a lot of nutrition in a small amount | Grains such as corn and barley, soybean meal, wheat bran, compound feed |

- Cows are grass-eating animals by nature. But to give a lot of milk they need more energy than grass alone can give, so farmers add concentrate
- **Too much concentrate** causes rumen acidosis and hoof problems (Chapters 2 and 11). **Too much roughage** means not enough energy and less milk
- Getting this **balance** right is the most basic rule of feeding

## Concentrate

| Group | Japanese | Examples | Main nutrient |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grains | 穀類 | Corn (maize), barley, wheat, milo (sorghum) | Starch (energy) |
| Oilseed meals | 油かす | Soybean meal, rapeseed meal, whole cottonseed | Protein |
| Bran | ぬか・ふすま | Rice bran, wheat bran | Fiber, protein, phosphorus |
| By-products | 食品工場のかす | Beet pulp, brewers' grains, tofu residue (okara), soy hulls | Easily digested fiber and more |
| Vitamins and minerals | ビタミン・ミネラル | Calcium carbonate, salt, vitamin mixes | Bones, milk, body functions |

- Whole grains are hard to digest, so they are steamed and pressed (flaked) or ground before feeding

## Compound feed

- **Compound feed** (配合飼料, also called formulated feed): feed made in a factory by mixing many ingredients to match a cow's needs. It comes as pellets, mash (powder) or flakes
- Example ingredients: corn, soybean meal, wheat bran, rapeseed meal, calcium carbonate, salt, vitamins, minerals
- The bag or delivery slip shows numbers like "TDN ○%, CP ○%" — the energy and protein content
- There are types for milking cows, dry cows, heifers and calves (starter)
- A feed made of only one ingredient is called a **single feed ingredient** (単味飼料)

### Compound feed and imports

- Almost all ingredients for compound feed in Japan are imported (corn comes from the USA, Brazil and other countries)
- So world grain prices, exchange rates and shipping costs change feed prices a lot (this is often in the news)
- Japan grows only about a quarter of all its livestock feed, and only a little over 10% of its concentrate
- Feed is the biggest cost on a dairy farm, close to half of all costs

## Roughage

- Grass, corn and rice are fed fresh, dried (hay, Chapter 6) or fermented (silage, Chapter 7)
- Some roughage is grown on the farm (home-grown feed), and some is imported hay
- **The better the roughage, the less concentrate you need.** Feed costs go down and cows stay healthier

## TMR and component feeding

| Method | Japanese | Description | Strengths and cautions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Component feeding | 分離給与 | Roughage and concentrate are fed separately. Common in tie-stall barns | Easy to change the amount for each cow. A big meal of concentrate can cause acidosis, so it is split into 3–6 meals a day |
| TMR (total mixed ration) | TMR | Roughage, concentrate and minerals are all mixed together. Common in free-stall barns | Every bite has the same nutrients, so the rumen stays stable. The mix is changed for each group of cows |

- A factory that makes TMR for several farms in an area is called a **TMR center**
- Cows only eat feed they can reach, so the feed they push away is pushed back (**feed pushing**; there are robots for this)
- Cows sometimes pick out the parts they like (concentrate). This is called **sorting** (選び食い). Chop length and moisture help prevent it

## Dry matter and nutrient words

| Word | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry matter (DM) | 乾物 | What is left without water. Feed amounts and nutrients are compared on a dry matter basis |
| Dry matter intake (DMI) | 乾物摂取量 | Dry matter eaten in a day. 3–4% of body weight (20–26 kg for a 650 kg cow) |
| TDN (total digestible nutrients) | TDN | The total of digestible nutrients. A measure of energy |
| Crude protein (CP) | 粗たんぱく質 | The amount of protein |
| NDF (neutral detergent fiber) | NDF | The amount of fiber. Too much makes feed bulky so cows eat less. Too little causes acidosis |
| Forage-to-concentrate ratio | 粗濃比 | The share of roughage and concentrate (on a dry matter basis). About 50:50 for high-yielding cows. Concentrate should not go above about 60% |

![Dry matter and water in 10 kg of feed](images/dry-matter.svg)

Typical moisture: hay 10–15%, silage 60–70%, compound feed 12–13%. For example, 10 kg of silage with 65% moisture is 3.5 kg of dry matter.

## Summary

- Feed is either fiber-rich **roughage** or nutrient-dense **concentrate**. Balance is the most important thing
- **Compound feed** is concentrate made by mixing grains, oilseed meals, minerals and more in a factory. Most ingredients are imported
- Feeding methods: **component feeding** (separately) and **TMR** (all mixed)
- Feed amounts and nutrients are compared as **dry matter** (without water)
