# 4. Raising calves

## What you will learn

- What to do right after birth
- Why colostrum is so important
- Milk feeding and weaning
- Common calf diseases
- Raising heifers

## Right after birth

- Check that the calf is breathing and that its nose and mouth are clear of fluid
- Disinfect the navel (dip it in iodine) to stop germs getting in
- In cold weather, dry the calf and keep it warm with straw or a calf jacket
- On dairy farms, calves are usually separated from the mother soon and raised in a calf hutch or pen
- An ear tag with a 10-digit individual ID number is attached (Chapter 12)

## Colostrum

- **Colostrum** (初乳): the first milk after calving. It is thick and yellow and full of **antibodies** (immunoglobulin, IgG)
- Calves are born with no antibodies (in cattle, antibodies cannot pass from the mother through the placenta). **A calf gets its first protection against disease from colostrum**
- The gut can absorb antibodies whole for only about 24 hours after birth. Absorption gets worse every hour

![Time after birth and the ability to absorb antibodies](images/colostrum.svg)

| Key point | Guide |
| --- | --- |
| Quickly | Within 2 hours after birth (6 hours at the latest) |
| Enough | About 10% of body weight (about 4 L) |
| Good quality | 22% or more on a Brix refractometer means plenty of antibodies |

- If there is not enough, or the quality is poor, use frozen colostrum or a colostrum product
- Calves that did not get enough colostrum get diarrhea and pneumonia more often, and more of them die

## Milk feeding

- After colostrum, calves drink whole milk or **milk replacer** (代用乳, powdered milk for calves), for example twice a day
- From early on, give **calf starter** (スターター, concentrate feed for calves), **water** and a little good hay. Eating solid feed makes the rumen grow (Chapter 2)
- Some farms use an automatic calf feeder, which gives each calf its own amount of milk

## Weaning

- **Weaning** (離乳): stopping milk so the calf eats only solid feed
- It is often done at 2–3 months old. When a calf eats 1 kg or more of starter a day for several days in a row, milk is reduced bit by bit and then stopped
- Around weaning, changes in feed and group come together and cause a lot of stress, so do not change many things at once

## Calf diseases

| Disease | Japanese | Signs | Example causes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diarrhea (scours) | 下痢 | Watery manure, weak, dehydrated (sunken eyes) | E. coli, rotavirus, coronavirus, Cryptosporidium, feeding mistakes |
| Pneumonia | 肺炎 | Cough, runny nose, fever, fast breathing | Viruses and bacteria, plus cold, drafts, poor ventilation and stress |
| Navel infection | へその炎症 | Swollen, hot navel, pus | The navel was not disinfected well at birth |

- The danger of diarrhea is dehydration. Give water and salts (electrolytes) by mouth. In bad cases a vet gives a drip
- Most calf diseases can be reduced with **colostrum, cleanliness, good ventilation and protection from cold**

## Heifers

- The goal: first insemination at **13–15 months** when the heifer weighs 350–400 kg (55–60% of adult weight), and first calving at **22–24 months** (Chapter 3)
- Heifers should gain about 0.8 kg a day
- Slow growth delays the first calving, and feed costs pile up before she gives any milk. On the other hand, making young heifers too fat is said to be bad for udder development
- Some farms send heifers to a local rearing farm or public pasture

## Summary

- After birth: check breathing, disinfect the navel, keep the calf warm
- Colostrum: "quickly (within 2 hours), enough (4 L), good quality". A calf's immunity depends on colostrum
- Raise calves on milk replacer and starter, and wean at 2–3 months
- Diarrhea and pneumonia are common. Prevent them with colostrum, cleanliness and ventilation
- First insemination at 13–15 months, first calving at 22–24 months
