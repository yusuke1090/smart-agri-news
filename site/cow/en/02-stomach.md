# 2. Four stomachs and rumination

## What you will learn

- What each of the four stomachs does
- What rumination is, and why it matters
- Rumen microbes, and how feed becomes milk
- Rumen acidosis

## The four stomachs

![The four stomachs and the flow of feed](images/stomach.svg)

| Stomach | Japanese | Name as food in Japan | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rumen | 第一胃 | ミノ (mino) | The largest (150–200 L in an adult). A "fermentation tank" where microbes ferment the feed |
| Reticulum | 第二胃 | ハチノス (hachinosu) | Joined to the rumen and mixes its contents. The gateway for sending food back to the mouth (rumination). Swallowed metal tends to collect here |
| Omasum | 第三胃 | センマイ (senmai) | Has many thin folds. Absorbs water and lets only small particles pass |
| Abomasum | 第四胃 | ギアラ (giara) | Works like a human stomach. Digests food with acid and enzymes |

- The four stomachs fill most of the left side of the belly. The rumen is about 80% of the total
- In a newborn calf, the abomasum is the largest and the rumen is still small. Milk goes straight to the abomasum through a channel called the esophageal groove (食道溝), skipping the rumen. When the calf starts eating grass and solid feed, the rumen grows (Chapter 4)

## Rumination

- **Rumination** (反芻): bringing swallowed food back up to the mouth and chewing it again. People also say "chewing the cud"
- Cows ruminate for **7–10 hours a day** (400–600 minutes), mostly while lying down and resting
- Each ball of food (a bolus) is chewed 40–70 times and then swallowed again
- Chewing makes a lot of saliva: 100–180 L a day
- Saliva contains something like baking soda (sodium bicarbonate). It stops the rumen from becoming too acidic (this is called buffering)

### Why rumination matters

- It breaks grass into small pieces, so microbes can digest it more easily
- Saliva keeps the rumen pH stable
- **Less rumination is a sign that something is wrong.** It drops with illness, calving, heat (estrus), hot weather and stress. That is why collars and other sensors measure rumination time (Chapter 12)
- If the diet has too little grass (fiber), cows ruminate less → make less saliva → the rumen becomes acidic (see "Rumen acidosis" below)

## Rumen microbes

- Bacteria, protozoa and fungi live in the rumen. There are more than 10 billion bacteria in 1 mL of rumen fluid
- Cows cannot digest grass fiber (cellulose) by themselves. **Microbes break it down and ferment it**, and the cow lives on what they make
- Fermentation makes **volatile fatty acids** (VFAs, 揮発性脂肪酸). VFAs supply about 60–70% of the cow's energy

| VFA | Japanese | Increased by | In the body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acetic acid (acetate) | 酢酸 | Grass (fiber) | Becomes milk fat |
| Propionic acid (propionate) | プロピオン酸 | Grain (starch) | Becomes glucose in the liver → lactose → milk volume |
| Butyric acid (butyrate) | 酪酸 | Sugar and starch | Feeds the rumen wall |

- The microbes themselves are later digested in the abomasum and small intestine and become protein for the cow (microbial protein)
- Fermentation also makes methane gas, which cows burp out. One cow makes hundreds of liters a day. Research to reduce it is active, because methane is a greenhouse gas (it is often in the news)

## How feed becomes milk

1. The cow bites off grass and swallows it with little chewing
2. Microbes ferment it in the rumen and reticulum (making VFAs and more microbes)
3. The cow brings it back up and chews it again (rumination, many times)
4. Small particles move on to the omasum → abomasum → small intestine, where they are digested and absorbed
5. VFAs are absorbed through the rumen wall, and other nutrients through the small intestine, into the blood
6. The blood carries the nutrients to the udder, where they become milk (Chapter 8)

## Rumen acidosis

- **Rumen acidosis** (ルーメンアシドーシス): a condition where the rumen becomes too acidic
- If a cow eats a lot of grain (concentrate) suddenly → starch ferments fast and makes a lot of acid → the rumen pH falls
- Normal rumen pH is 6–7. When it stays below 5.8 for long periods, it is **subacute rumen acidosis** (SARA). Below 5.0 it is acute and can kill the cow

| Sign | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Eats unevenly, ruminates less | The rumen is not working well |
| Loose manure with bubbles or undigested grain | Feed is not fully digested in the rumen |
| Lower milk fat | Less acetic acid |
| More hoof problems | Acids and toxins travel through the body and damage the hooves (Chapter 11) |

To prevent it: feed enough grass (fiber), do not feed a lot of concentrate at once (split it into several meals, or use a TMR), and do not change the diet suddenly (Chapter 5).

## Summary

- Cows have four stomachs. The rumen is a fermentation tank full of microbes
- Cows ruminate 7–10 hours a day. Saliva protects the rumen. Less rumination is a sign of trouble
- Microbes ferment grass into VFAs, which give the cow energy and become milk
- Too much grain causes rumen acidosis. Balance it with grass
